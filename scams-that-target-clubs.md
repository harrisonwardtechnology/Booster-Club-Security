# Scams That Target Clubs

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. None of these are hypothetical. All of them are running right now, against organizations exactly like yours.

**Purpose.** The actual repertoire aimed at volunteer treasurers, and the counter for each, which is nearly always the same counter.

## The President Needs Gift Cards

The classic, tuned for volunteer orgs: an email or text to the treasurer, appearing to be the president or a board member, needing gift cards for volunteer thank-yous or an urgent payment, quietly, today, they are in a meeting and cannot call. In 2026 the voice version exists too, and it sounds exactly like your president, because a few seconds of audio from any concert livestream is enough to clone anyone. The counter never changes: real requests survive a callback to the number you already have, gift cards are never how organizations pay for anything, and urgent plus secret equals scam. Boards that want the extra lock borrow the [code word](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/Verify-Before-You-Pay) from the business world, ten minutes at one meeting to set up.

## The Fake or Redirected Invoice

An invoice arrives that looks exactly like your uniform vendor, trip company, or equipment supplier, real logos, plausible amounts, sometimes referencing the actual order, with new payment details. Either pure fabrication or, worse, the vendor's real email was compromised and the real thread now carries a bad account number. The counter: [new payees and changed details always get the callback](the-treasurers-page.md) at a known number, and big seasonal payments, the spring trip, the uniform order, deserve the call even when nothing changed, thirty seconds against five figures.

## The Compromised Club Account

A club Gmail or social account gets phished, and the attacker uses the club's own trusted voice: fundraiser posts with swapped payment links, dues reminders pointing at fake pages, messages to parents that carry the club's full credibility. The counter is [the accounts page](accounts-and-handover.md) wholesale, MFA and role ownership, plus one habit for everyone else: parents paying the club should expect payments only through the channels announced at meetings and in the newsletter, and any new payment link that arrives by DM deserves a question.

## The Fundraising Platform Look-Alike

Emails impersonating the club's fundraising or ticketing platform, your payout failed, verify your bank details, aimed at whoever administers the campaign. The counter: platform business goes through the platform, log in by typing the address, never through emailed links, and payout detail changes get the same two-person treatment as any other [money movement](the-treasurers-page.md).

## The Overpayment and the Refund

A generous check for the fundraiser arrives, too generous, followed by a request to refund the difference, and the original check bounces after your real money left. Any overpayment plus refund request is the scam itself; there is no innocent version at club scale. Funds are not funds until the bank confirms final settlement, which takes longer than the scammer wants you to think.

## Why Clubs, and the One Habit Again

Fraudsters target volunteer orgs precisely because the roster is public, the officers are listed on the website with emails, the fundraising calendar announces when money is moving, and the treasurer is a tired parent doing this at night. You cannot and should not hide any of that, transparency is club life. The defense is not secrecy, it is process: [two people, callbacks, known numbers](the-treasurers-page.md), and a board where saying "I checked first" earns thanks, never eye-rolls. Say that last part out loud at a meeting once a year, it is the whole culture in one sentence.
