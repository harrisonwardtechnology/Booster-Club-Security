# Families and Kids Data

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. The club holds more sensitive data than most small businesses: children's names, homes, health notes, and schedules. Hold it like you know that.

**Purpose.** Keeping less, guarding what you keep, and posting in ways that never make a kid findable.

## Keep Less, the Master Control

Every form the club creates should collect the minimum the activity needs, and the strongest privacy program available to a volunteer org is simply not having data: no birthdates where a grade level works, no addresses where an email works, medical details only for events that genuinely need them, held only until the event ends. Data you never collected cannot leak, cannot be mishandled by a well-meaning volunteer, and cannot become the club's worst email to write. At each year's end, delete what the year no longer needs, old rosters, old medical forms, old payment spreadsheets, and make the deletion a calendar item in the [handover checklist](accounts-and-handover.md), not a hope.

## One Home Per Kind of Data

The failure mode is nine spreadsheets: the roster in six volunteers' downloads folders, medical forms photographed into a group chat, the payment tracker mailed around as attachments. The fix is one club-owned location per kind of data, in the club's accounts from [the accounts page](accounts-and-handover.md), shared to roles that need it and nobody else, with medical information held tightest, the trip chaperone lead and the nurse-parent, not the whole committee. Attachments and group texts are how club data escapes; links to the one home, with access controlled, are how it stays.

## Posting Without Making Kids Findable

The club's public life is real, concerts, competitions, celebrations, and the rule that keeps it safe is about combinations: a face, a full name, and a predictable location and time, together, in public, is the combination to avoid. First names or section names in captions, group shots over tagged close-ups for the public feed, honor every family's photo permission preference without making the opting-out kid conspicuous, and hold trip itineraries with dates, hotels, and rosters to the traveling families rather than the public page. Publish the schedule of the band, not the whereabouts of a child, the distinction sounds fine until you try it, and then it becomes easy.

## Volunteers See What Their Job Needs

The [least-privilege idea](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/Least-Privilege-and-RBAC) at bake-sale scale: the concessions coordinator does not need the medical forms, the photographer does not need the roster, and access gets granted by role and removed at [handover](accounts-and-handover.md). Not suspicion, dignity: volunteers who can only reach what their job needs can never be part of any later question about anything else.

## If Data Gets Loose

A misdirected email, a lost binder, a compromised account: tell the affected families honestly and promptly, plain words, what got out, what you did, what you are watching for, and loop in the school where kids' data is involved, districts have obligations and would rather hear it from you early. The [incident instincts](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/IR-First-24-Hours) apply at club scale: contain, be truthful, do not speculate, and never let embarrassment delay the telling, the families' trust survives an honest mistake and does not survive a discovered silence.
