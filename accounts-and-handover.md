# Accounts and Handover

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. Every volunteer org has a June. This page is how the club survives it.

**Purpose.** Making the club's digital life belong to the club, so officer transitions transfer a working organization instead of a scavenger hunt.

## The June Problem

Volunteer orgs turn over their entire leadership on a schedule, and the digital estate usually lives wherever the outgoing people happened to put it: the website registered to a parent who graduated out three years ago, the Instagram recovery number pointing at someone's old phone, the bank login only the former treasurer knows, the Gmail password in a text thread from 2023. Every one of those is a future lockout, and lockouts of club accounts are functionally breaches, the club loses control of its own name, money visibility, and communication channels.

## Accounts Belong to Roles

The fix is ownership by role, not person: club email addresses like treasurer@ or president@ on the club's own domain or a club-owned account, socials registered to club email, the website and domain in the club's name with two officers as contacts, and the bank relationship documented so the signers change with the officers, which your bank does routinely if asked. The test for every account: if this volunteer moved away tomorrow, does the club still hold the keys, and the inventory that answers it is one page, every account, what it is for, which role owns it, listed in the binder.

## The Club Password Manager

A shared password manager, free tiers cover a small board, holds every club credential, organized so each officer sees what their role needs. MFA goes on everything that supports it, anchored to club-owned contact points plus two current officers as recovery, never one. This single tool converts the June handover from archaeology into administration: access is granted to incoming officers and removed from outgoing ones in an afternoon, and no password ever again lives only in somebody's head or kitchen drawer. The [home version of this habit](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/Security-Starts-at-Home) is the same move at family scale, and board members who do one tend to do the other.

## The Handover Checklist

At transition, same week, not same semester: incoming officers added to the password manager and outgoing removed, bank signers updated with the bank, MFA and recovery contacts moved to current officers, admin rights on socials, website, email, and fundraising platforms transferred and old access removed, and the inventory page walked together, outgoing and incoming, once. Removal is the forgotten half, the former officer who still has posting rights two years later is almost always innocent and occasionally the story, and clean removal protects them from ever being part of the question, the [same dignity logic](the-treasurers-page.md) as the two-person rule.

## The Annual Ten-Minute Audit

Each fall, one officer spends ten minutes against the inventory: does every account still exist, do current officers hold access, do any former officers, are recovery contacts current. Ten minutes, because the inventory made it possible, and the club that does this never gets to find out what the alternative costs.
