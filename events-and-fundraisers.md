# Events and Fundraisers

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. Game night moves more cash through more hands than anything else the club does. A little structure keeps it fun.

**Purpose.** Security for the nights the club actually handles money in public: concessions, ticket tables, auctions, and the QR codes taped to everything.

## Cash With Two Humans

Concessions and ticket cash follows one rule end to end: two unrelated people count it, together, at the start, at shift changes, and at close, on a one-page count sheet both sign, and the bank deposit happens fast, next business day, not after a week in someone's trunk. The [two-person logic](the-treasurers-page.md) again, and again it protects the volunteers first: nobody who counted with a partner and signed a sheet can ever be part of a discrepancy story alone. Cash boxes get owners by name per shift, and startup change gets counted in writing too, because that is where honest confusion loves to live.

## QR Codes Are the New Cash Box

Payment QR codes taped to tables are wonderful and swappable: a sticker over your code redirects a night's payments to a stranger, and nobody looks closely at a QR code, which is the entire attack. The counters are cheap: print codes fresh per event, put them in frames or laminated stands that make tampering visible, have one volunteer verify each code scans to the right destination at setup and once mid-event, and announce the official payment names from the stage, "we take payments as FMHS-style club name on the platform," so a wrong name in a parent's app gets noticed in the moment. Payment platforms for events use the club's business account per [the accounts page](accounts-and-handover.md), never a volunteer's personal one, however convenient the night makes it sound.

## Card Readers and the Table Setup

Square-style readers travel in custody like the cash box: signed out by name, reconciled per shift in the platform's own reports, and logged into with the club account, not whoever's phone was handy. Venue wifi is fine for card readers, they encrypt their business, but the club's admin work, refunds, payout changes, account settings, waits for a trusted connection at home rather than the gym's open network, a small discipline that costs nothing.

## Ticketing and Auction Platforms

Sell only through the platforms the club announced, tell families exactly what the official links look like in the newsletter, and treat any last-minute link change request with the [same suspicion as a bank change](scams-that-target-clubs.md), because scalpers and scammers both love a hot event. Auction and raffle nights carry one extra caution that is not security at all: state rules on raffles and gaming vary and are enforced, so a five-minute call to your state association before the fun is the cheapest compliance program in this repo, and this is the [not-legal-advice line](DISCLAIMER.md) in its natural habitat.

## The Post-Event Fifteen Minutes

While chairs are being folded: counts reconciled against platform reports, cash bagged with the sheet, readers and boxes checked in, QR frames collected, and one line of notes about anything odd, the person who asked strange questions about the payment setup, the code that looked peeled at table six. Odd notes across events become patterns, and patterns are how volunteer orgs, who share everything else at district roundtables, should share this too.
